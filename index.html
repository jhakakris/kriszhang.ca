What I'm working on

Where should AI data centers be built?

Data centers need huge amounts of electricity, and the water used to cool them and to generate that electricity can strain local supplies. I'm building a siting model that asks whether a location still looks good when water stress, grid decarbonization and cooling technology all change at once, instead of assuming today's conditions last.

Data: IM3 Data Center Atlas · WRI Aqueduct 4.0 · EPA eGRID

Speed to power, and who pays for it

New data centers can wait years to connect to the grid. I study how interconnection rules and large-load tariffs shape how fast they get power, and what that growth means for utility costs, household electricity bills and location-based emissions.

Loan-ly Planet

A climate risk monitor for agricultural lenders.

Banks finance thousands of farms but lose sight of them once the loan is paid out. Loan-ly Planet compares each field's satellite and weather signals with its own history to flag drought, flooding, frost and heat, explains why a loan is at risk, and shows the effect on the bank's loan-loss provisions under IFRS 9. Demonstrated on a 240-loan sample portfolio replaying the 2022/23 La Niña drought in South America.

Watt If

A power planner for a new data-center campus.

A developer sets a cost ceiling and a carbon ceiling; Watt If finds the earliest month a 75 MW campus can open while meeting both, and the energy mix that gets it there. Behind it are 155,893 combinations of solar, wind, nuclear, batteries and gas, each simulated hour by hour over ten years under a grid connection that arrives in stages. You can open any plan and see every hour of it.

Climate-informed mortality modelling for insurance and financial risk

Directed research, University of Waterloo · Mentor: Rhoda Dadzie-Dennis

Can climate signals improve long-horizon mortality forecasts, and what does that mean for insurers and financial risk? I extended the Lee–Carter mortality model with mean annual temperature using Ontario data (1991–2023) and compared it with the standard model out of sample. Adding temperature improved fit and gave more decision-relevant long-term signals under warming trends.

How cells sense their environment

Directed research, University of Waterloo · Mentor: Gordon R. McNicol

A mathematical model of how cells feel the stiffness of the material around them. I analysed a system of 18 coupled equations, ran sensitivity and drug dose-response experiments, and found switch-like behaviour in how cells commit to adhering. Presented at the Directed Reading Programme symposium.

Machine-checked proofs for optimization

Directed research, University of Waterloo · Mentor: Sita Gakkhar

Using the Lean 4 proof assistant, I built the mathematical pieces behind linear programming and completed a computer-verified proof of the Weak Duality Theorem, a check that the maths behind an optimization model is airtight.
